## 1. What phase 4 is for

The article's focus is now the engineering problem the lead stated on 2026-10-23: engineers
turning circuit designs into physical ones on IBM hardware do not reach the fidelity they
expect, and Aer's stock noise model, built from the latest calibration reading, is not a useful
guide. The engine's job is to be a materially better design-time predictor: which qubits, how
deep, what fidelity, with an honest range.

Today's evidence narrows what that predictor must be:

- per qubit, not chip-uniform (a week-stale per-qubit channel beats a fresh uniform one,
  NC-083);
- centred on each qubit's calibration **level**, not its latest reading (shrinkage to level
  explains 44% of the next change, the latest reading none, NC-085; the rolling band beats the
  persistence band everywhere, NC-081);
- with a **range** whose width is the measured scatter (about 0.37 log units for T1 and T2,
  larger for readout, NC-084 to NC-086);
- with no forecaster behind it from calibration history alone (the pre-registered rule for
  deep-model work failed on all three features), and with readout's chip-wide component as
  the one place same-time cross-sectional information is large (NC-086).

Whether the level or the range is the product is decided by the hardware test (#125). The
fuzzy and IT2 layer is the interpretable machinery that produces the band; its novelty as a
calibration-to-channel map stands (novelty check §3.4, §3.5).

## 4. What the team stops doing

- No new deep-model work until #125 reports and the chip-level readout series has a
  measured predictability of its own.
- No new measurements beyond #125 and lane 1 item 5.
- No new ADRs until #109 flips the ones already waiting (ADR-009, ADR-011, ADR-014 to
  ADR-016, ADR-019, ADR-021, ADR-025's amendments).
- No paper sentence about drift bracketing. The sentence the evidence supports is: noise
  models should carry the calibration level and its range, per qubit; here is how much better
  that is than the latest reading, on the archive and on hardware.

## 6. Dates, as targets and not as registered claims

Phase 3 closes on 2026-10-31 as planned. Phase 4 runs from 2026-11-01. The gates above are
ordered, not dated, because every one of them depends on a measurement whose outcome is not
known: L1 within two weeks of job 1's submission if the Open Plan allowance holds; L3 within
the month after Bengisu's answers; L2 and L4 before the end of the year if nothing in lanes 1
and 2 slips; the first draft (lane 2's paper, with lane 1's hardware section if L2 passes)
in the first quarter of 2027. A Q1 review cycle is long; a submission before spring is the
realistic target, and the phase-3 plan's own §6 is the precedent for writing that down
rather than a date.
